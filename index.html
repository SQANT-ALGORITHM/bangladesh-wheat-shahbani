Chapter 01

Executive Summary

A consolidated read of the DGF Package-03 milling wheat tender, its award outcome, and what it signals for global exporters seeking access to Bangladesh's public grain procurement channel.

Key Finding

Bangladesh's Directorate General of Food (DGF) awarded Package-03 (50,000 MT ±5% milling wheat, FY2025-26) to Agrocorp International Pte Ltd (Singapore) at US$297.92/MT CIF Liner Out (Chattogram 60% / Mongla 40%), confirming a persistent pattern: Agrocorp holds a dominant, near-gatekeeper position in Bangladesh's government wheat channel, combining open-tender wins with G2G supply via its US Wheat Associates relationship. For global traders sourcing from origin millers, the most capital-efficient entry point is not direct tender participation but back-to-back supply into Agrocorp's award pipeline, run in parallel with direct outreach to Bangladesh's large private millers.

Blacklist & Eligibility Rules (Tender Clause 30)
Incomplete, conditional, or alternate offers are not considered. In addition, a tenderer is ineligible to bid if: (i) it holds an unshipped award still awaiting contract or shipment sail date; (ii) it previously won an award but failed to submit the Performance Guarantee on time; (iii) its bid-bond was forfeited twice in the current financial year for non-submission of PG or non-signing of contract; or (iv) its full PG was forfeited for non-supply — in both (iii) and (iv), ineligibility extends for the rest of the financial year regardless of whether forfeited funds were deposited to Treasury. Execution discipline is not optional — it directly gates future tender access.

Chapter 04

Agrocorp Market Positioning

Why Agrocorp functions as the practical gatekeeper of Bangladesh's government wheat channel — and what that means for upstream suppliers.

✓ Strengths

  • Repeated award history in DGF open tenders
  • Established G2G channel via US Wheat Associates MOU
  • Working capital and LC structuring (95/5 split) already in place
  • Demonstrated execution track record with DGF, reducing blacklist risk

✗ Structural Dependencies

  • Physical grain must still be sourced upstream from origin millers
  • Landed cost benchmark (~$298–$322/MT) sets a ceiling competitors can undercut
  • Reliant on timely origin sourcing to meet 25-day shipment windows

◆ Opportunity for Upstream Suppliers

  • Back-to-back FOB supply to Agrocorp's origination desk (Singapore/Canada/Australia-focused)
  • Trial cargo entry point: 25,000–50,000 MT to prove execution
  • No need for Bangladesh IRC/importer registration to participate indirectly

⚠ Watch Items

  • Agrocorp's incumbency advantage makes direct-tender competition difficult for new entrants
  • Relationship-based allocation favors proven, existing suppliers to Agrocorp
  • Government award cadence is irregular, not calendar-based

Chapter 06

Logistics, Payment & Security Terms

Commercial mechanics governing DGF wheat tenders — critical for structuring any competitive bid or back-to-back supply offer.

Discharge Ports
Chattogram (60% of volume) and Mongla (40% of volume), on CIF Liner Out terms. Cost of cargo, insurance, freight, stevedoring, overside handling, and lightering all sit on the seller's account.
Tender Security
US$300,000 per bid — Bank Draft or Pay Order only. No Bank Guarantee accepted. Refundable for non-winners; forfeited on default.
Payment Mechanism
Letter of Credit (L/C). Shipment must occur within 25 days of L/C opening — a tight window that rewards suppliers with pre-positioned or fast-moving origin stock.
Document & Eligibility Cost
Tender document: Tk 5,000 or USD 100 (non-refundable). Eligibility requires complete, unconditional offers — no alternates.
Chapter 07

Strategic Entry Pathways

Three viable routes into the Bangladesh wheat market for a global exporter/trader sourcing from origin millers, ranked by speed and risk.

A

Back-to-Back Supply to Agrocorp (Recommended Primary Path)

Position as a reliable FOB supplier into Agrocorp's origination desk (Singapore/Canada/Australia-focused), leveraging origin miller relationships (Argentina, Brazil, US, Canada, Black Sea) and pricing below Agrocorp's landed benchmark of ~$298–322/MT. Target a 25,000–50,000 MT trial cargo to prove execution. Avoids direct tender risk, deposits, and blacklist exposure while using Agrocorp's established L/C structure (95/5 split) and DGF relationship.

High Priority
B

Private Channel — Direct to Major Millers (Recommended Parallel Path)

Approach major private flour/feed millers directly — Meghna Group, Akij Insaf Group, TK Group, and BPICC members — who collectively handle the majority of total wheat/maize import volume outside DGF tenders. No tender security required; direct negotiation; matches bi-monthly scale sourcing models (e.g., 60,000 MT). Faster entry than the government channel.

Medium Priority — Run in Parallel
C

Direct Tender Bidding (Higher Commitment, Longer Runway)

Open to "bonafide traders" globally in principle, but requires a Bangladesh-registered agent or Import Registration Certificate (IRC) for full participation, plus a US$300,000 tender security per bid (bank draft/pay order only) and clean compliance against strict blacklist rules. Competing directly against an entrenched incumbent like Agrocorp raises execution and pricing risk. Relationship maintenance with the Director (Procurement) / CCGP is useful for status visibility but is not a guaranteed fast-track.

Lower Priority — Longer-Term Option
Immediate Action Plan
1) Contact DGF Procurement Director Md. Moniruzzaman (dproc@dgfood.gov.bd, +88-02-41050178) for Package-03 execution confirmation and pipeline visibility.
2) Prepare spec-compliant, competitively priced FOB offers from qualifying origins.
3) Draft parallel introductions to Agrocorp's origination desk and to private millers (Meghna, Akij Insaf, TK Group, BPICC).
4) Start with a trial cargo to establish clean execution history before scaling volume.
5) Model freight and insurance costs to beat the $297–$322/MT landed benchmark.
Chapter 08

Risk Assessment

Key risk factors across execution, compliance, and market-access dimensions.

High Risk
  • Missing the 25-day shipment window post-L/C opening
  • Bid-bond or Performance Guarantee forfeiture triggering multi-tender ban
  • Quality rejection on strict specs (esp. 0.7% foreign material ceiling)
Medium Risk
  • Direct tender competition against entrenched incumbent (Agrocorp)
  • Irregular, non-calendar tender cadence complicating supply planning
  • FX and freight volatility eroding margin against fixed CIF benchmarks
Low Risk
  • Back-to-back FOB supply to an established, already-qualified awardee
  • Private-channel sales to major millers outside the tender process
  • Relationship-building follow-up with DGF Procurement Division
Regulatory Watch
  • Origin restriction: all countries eligible except Israel
  • Crop-year requirement: 2026 or latest
  • Blacklist rules apply per financial year, not per tender — track record compounds
